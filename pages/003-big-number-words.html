Nine hundred and ninety nine (more) Thousands

Then thousands (also each called grand, which is a collective singular term) each contain nine hundreds and the first set of $$ \begin{bmatrix} 13 & 7 & 8 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} \text{primary colors} \\ \text{-teens} \\ \text{groups of ten} \end{bmatrix} $$ which total the missing hundred to form the ten hundreds that are a thousand. The thousand's place, ten‑thousand's place, and hundred‑thousand's place are separated from the hundreds, tens, and ones by a comma, so \( 1,000 = \text{one thousand} \) and \( 999,009 = \text{ nine hundred and ninetey‑nine thousand and nine }\).

$$\begin{aligned} &\text{[one thousand .. one thousand nine hundred ninety-nine].} \\ &\text{[two grand .. two grand nine hundred ninety-nine].} \\ &\text{.} \\ &\text{[nine grand .. nine grand nine hundred ninety-nine].} \\ &\text{.} \\ &\text{.} \\ &\text{[one hundred thousand .. one hundred thousand nine hundred ninety-nine].} \\ &\text{.} \\ &\text{.} \\ &\text{.} \\ &\text{[nine hundred and ninety-nine thousand .. 999,999].} \end{aligned}$$

You can have two grand, one hundred grand ... nine hundred ninety-nine grand, and for each its hundreds, tens and ones.

-illions

Then millions, billions, trillions, quadrillions, quintillions, sextillions, septillions, octillions, nonillions.
You can have one million .. nine hundred ninety-nine million, and then the billion is one grand *times* more than one million in a *multiplicative* sense.
