Thirteen distinct counting numbers ("primary colors")

We have these names that we use for the first ten counting numbers: $$ \text{\{one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten\}} $$ Verify that there are ten fingers on your two hands. In English there are more than ten distinct numbers:
$$ \text{\{eleven, twelve\}} $$ There are twelve distinct numbers.
In fact, there are thirteen distinct numbers if you count zero (0). That is, if you add zero to the set of unique counting numbers and take its cardinality. So now we have thirteen primary "colors" with which we can "paint" the counting numbers, along with a set of modifiers. We use only forms of one through ten for writing numbers less than or equal to 99, after which we introduce modifiers like hundred, thousand, and m‑illion, the last of which comes with its own modifiers like b-, tr-, and quadr-, for two‑, three‑, or four‑thousand times more than one million. $$\begin{aligned} &\{ \text{zero,} \\ &\text{one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten,} \\ &\text{eleven, twelve} \} \end{aligned}$$

Seven "-teens"

Then there are the seven -teens, which makes 20 numbers total. $$\begin{aligned} \text{[thirteen, fourteen, fifteen, sixteen, } \text{seventeen, eighteen, nineteen].} \end{aligned}$$ These next seven numbers are of the form $$ 10 + [3..9]. $$ The new forms of the numbers one through ten include "thir" for three, "fif" for five, and "teen" for ten.

Eight groups of ten

Once we get to [twenty], the counting numbers begin following a very predictable pattern: along with twenty, here are the next ten numbers after nineteen. $$\begin{aligned} \text{[twenty twenty-one twenty-two ... twenty-nine].} \end{aligned}$$ Notice how the twenties are just $$ 20 + [0..9]. $$ Also notice that [twenty] is twen-ty or two tens.
New forms include "twen" for two and "ty" for ten.
The next seventy numbers follow the same pattern, with new forms including "for" for four, and "eight" combining letters with "ty", which result in counting eight groups of ten. $$\begin{aligned} &\text{[thirty thirty-one thirty-two ... thirty-nine].} \\ &\text{[forty forty-one forty-two ... forty-nine].} \\ &\text{[fifty fifty-one fifty-two ... fifty-nine].} \\ &\text{[sixty sixty-one sixty-two ... sixty-nine].} \\ &\text{[seventy seventy-one seventy-two ... seventy-nine].} \\ &\text{[eighty eighty-one eighty-two ... eighty-nine].} \\ &\text{[ninety ninety-one ninety-two ... ninety-nine].} \end{aligned}$$

Nine (more) Hundreds

Then the nine hundreds appear, with each hundred containing a copy of each the thirteen distinct numbers, the seven -teens, and the eight groups of ten.

$$\begin{aligned} &\text{[one hundred .. one hundred ninety-nine].} \\ &\text{[two hundred .. two hundred ninety-nine].} \\ &\text{.} \\ &\text{.} \\ &\text{[nine hundred .. nine hundred ninety-nine].} \end{aligned}$$
